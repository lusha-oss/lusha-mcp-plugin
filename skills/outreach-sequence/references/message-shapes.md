# Message shapes: Email 2, Email 3 and LinkedIn

Each shape is a set of slots, never text to copy. Every message also follows `SKILL.md` 8c (signals in the copy) and 8e (copy rules), and opens with *Hi {first name},* (on its own line in an email). Word counts exclude the greeting; character counts include it.

## Email 2: one new reason, in Email 1's thread
- 40–80 words, never under 40, and shorter than Email 1. The `email_2_subject` column repeats Email 1's subject unchanged, so the sending tool threads it; never add "Re:".
- Sentence 1 is one new reason, the first of these that exists:
  1. a second signal that passes 8c and was not used in Email 1;
  2. a proof point from the brief, not used in Email 1, that fits this contact's industry or this persona's pain (the customer named only when marked *OK to name: yes*). Word it for this contact: lead with the problem it solved, in this persona's terms, then the result with its facts and figures exact. Never paste the brief's sentence as it is;
  3. what Email 1's reason means for this persona's pain, asked as a question.
- Then, optionally, that reason tied to the pain in one clause. End on one small ask, or a question about who else owns this topic.
- Never open on following up, on a previous email, or on not hearing back. An objection from the brief appears only when a signal shows it applies to this contact (for example, the prospect uses a competitor the brief lists), and never as the opening line.

## Email 3: the last email, closing the same thread
- 25–50 words, and shorter than Email 2. The `email_3_subject` column repeats Email 1's subject unchanged.
- Sentence 1 names Email 1's reason (its signal or the pain) in new words.
- Then either who else owns that topic, or a later moment tied to it, named as a month or an event.
- End on one easy question about the topic: who owns it, or whether that later moment suits them. Never a question about whether to keep writing.
- No re-pitch, no guilt, no stock closing line, nothing about not hearing back. There is no fourth email.

## LinkedIn message: once they accept (1st-degree)
- Under 400 characters, three sentences: who the user is (their first name, if known, and their company) with the reason as Email 1 defines it; what the product does, in one clause; one question about interest, never a sales ask.
- No proof points, figures (amounts, percentages, counts) or links. A name that contains digits stays as written.
- When LinkedIn messages are drafted without connection notes, show this line above the table, once, word for word, as its own line:
  `Send the connection requests without a note; a free account allows 3 notes a month.`

## LinkedIn connection note: only when the user asks for notes
- A note goes only to a contact with a first-hand fact or a signal that passes 8c. Every other contact gets no note, and the summary says to send their request blank.
- 140–200 characters, never over 200, three parts: the reason (the occasion the user stated, with what they discussed when the user said so, or the signal as the source states it); who the user is (name and company); why staying in touch fits (the topic discussed, else their role, in their terms).
- No pitch, link, figure, proof, compliment or ask. A bare wish to connect is not a reason to stay in touch.
- When the user says they met a contact but not what they discussed, write the note from the occasion and the contact's role, then ask once, word for word, as its own line: `Tell me what you talked about and I'll work it into the note.` *Automated:* do not ask.
